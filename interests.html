Interests

Subjects I keep coming back to, in no particular order.

Psychology

I'm interested in how people actually reason and decide, as opposed to how we assume they do. That gap shows up everywhere — in debate rounds, in how questions get written for a competition, in ordinary arguments. Most of what draws me to psychology is that gap, and what it implies about how information should be presented if you actually want someone to update their thinking.

Research

Less a single discipline than a habit: I'm interested in methods that let you actually test an idea rather than just argue for it well. Debate teaches you to make a case; research teaches you to check whether the case survives contact with evidence. I try to hold both at once.

OSINT

Open-source intelligence appeals to the same instinct as research: most answers already exist somewhere public, if you're patient and precise about how you look. I'm drawn to the discipline of verification it demands — treating a claim as unproven until you've traced it back yourself.

Literature

I read widely rather than in one genre, and I pay as much attention to how something is built — structure, voice, argument — as to what it says. [Add specific authors, genres, or works you'd like to name here.]

Writing

Writing is where I actually work out what I think about something, after research and debate have done the arguing. It's less a performance than a way of finding out whether an idea holds together once it's written down plainly.
